# Oath Inventory — Texas Deposition

What each oath is, who administers it, whether any authority publishes wording for it, and what the record asserts about it afterward.

**No changes have been made to any template, script, or brief on the basis of this report.** It is an inventory, produced so the decisions that follow are made against a list rather than against memory.

## Summary

There are **four** oaths or oath-substitutes in a Texas deposition as this application will encounter them, plus one modality question that conditions two of them.

| # | Oath | Published wording? | Wording as administered? | Where |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Interpreter — spoken language | **Yes**, suggested form | Not observed — no interpreter in any specimen | UFM §3.11 |
| 2 | Interpreter — sign language | **Yes**, suggested form | Not observed | UFM §3.11 |
| 3 | Witness oath | **No** | **Yes** — recovered verbatim, all three specimens | §Oath 3 |
| 4 | Witness affirmation | **No** | **Not observed** — never administered in any specimen | — |
| 5 | Remote administration | **No** — 2010 manual predates it | **Yes** — stipulation taken on the record, all three | §Oath 5 |

The distribution is the opposite of what the application needs. The two conditional interpreter oaths have published wording and have never been used here. The oath given at every deposition has no published wording, and its substitute has neither published wording nor any observed instance.

## Oath 1 — Interpreter, spoken language

**When:** after the record is opened, before the interpreter renders any speech, and therefore before the admonitions and before the witness oath.

**Administered by:** the deposition officer.

**Published wording:** yes. UFM §3.11, printed page 15, in a box:

> **Suggested Oath to the Interpreter:**
>
> COURT REPORTER: "Do you solemnly swear or affirm that the interpretation you will give in this deposition will be from English to Spanish/Vietnamese/etc. and from Spanish/Vietnamese/etc. to English to the best of your ability?"

**Tier — three-way split. Do not label this step with a single tier.**

| What | Tier | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| That the interpreter be sworn | REQUIRED-IN-SUBSTANCE | TRE 604. **Not read.** |
| The wording | **CUSTOMARY**, with a suggested form published by the Supreme Court | §3.11 box |
| That the transcript show it | MANDATORY | §3.11 body, Figure 16 |

Three indicators on the page establish that the wording is not prescribed: the heading says "Suggested"; the text sits in a box in reduced type, which the UFM preface at printed page 3 states does not prescribe the format or content of the record; and the mandatory sentence in §3.11 is about what the transcription shows, not about what the reporter says.

**Notes.**

- The 2003 edition included the interpreter's reply, `INTERPRETER: "I do."`, inside this block. The 2010 revision removed it. The reply is not part of the oath.
- "in this deposition" is in the published text. The form is deposition-specific as written.
- Any citation of **Figure 17** for this oath is wrong. Figure 17 is titled "QUESTIONS AND ANSWERS" and is a tab-setting example. The 2003 edition pointed to Figure 17; the 2010 edition points to Figure 16.

## Oath 2 — Interpreter, sign language

**When and administered by:** identical to Oath 1. Mutually exclusive with it in practice.

**Published wording:** yes. UFM §3.11, same box:

> **Suggested Oath to Sign Interpreter:**
>
> COURT REPORTER: "do you solemnly swear or affirm that the interpretation you will give in this deposition will be from English to American Sign Language/Signed English and American Sign Language/Signed English to English to the best of your ability?"

**Tier:** identical to Oath 1.

**Note — the source is typeset inconsistently.** The published form begins with a lowercase "do" where the spoken-language form begins with "Do". This is almost certainly a typographical error in the manual, but reproducing a published form faithfully and correcting a published form are different acts, and the second is a decision. Recorded as an open item, not fixed.

## Oath 3 — Witness oath

**When:** before examination begins. At every deposition.

**Administered by:** the deposition officer. A CSR is competent to administer oaths — the UFM §3.7 comment cites Gov't Code § 52.025(b) for that, and **that section number is stale.** Reporter regulation moved to Chapter 154 under the Judicial Branch Certification Commission. § 154.105 is titled "Title; Oaths" and appears to be the successor. **Its text has not been read.**

**Published wording: none.**

There is no witness oath text anywhere in the UFM body §§3.1–3.24, and none in any figure read for this report. The manual publishes a suggested form for the interpreter and publishes nothing for the witness.

**Wording as actually administered: recovered.** Contrary to the expectation that the manual's conventions would keep the oath out of the transcript, all three specimen records transcribe it verbatim. Etminan, Thomas and Baier carry the same wording:

> THE REPORTER: `[WITNESS NAME]`, would you please raise your right hand, `[sir/ma'am]`? Do you solemnly swear to tell the truth, the whole truth, and nothing but the truth, so help you God?

Expected response: "I do." (Etminan, Thomas) or "Yes, ma'am." (Baier). The response is transcribed and is not part of the oath.

Each record then carries the Figure 15 setup line — `having been first duly sworn, testified as follows:` followed by `EXAMINATION` — so the notation and the verbatim oath coexist rather than substituting for one another.

This is evidence of practice, not authority. It does not establish that the wording is legally sufficient. It does establish what the certificate in each of those records was certifying to, which is a stronger starting point than reconstruction.

**Tier:**

| What | Tier | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| That the witness be sworn | REQUIRED-IN-SUBSTANCE | TRE 603, TRCP 199.5(b), Gov't Code Ch. 154. **None read.** |
| The wording | **CUSTOMARY** so far as anything read here shows | No published form located |
| That the transcript show it | MANDATORY | §3.10, Figures 14 and 15 |
| That the certificate assert it | MANDATORY | Figures 8 and 9 |

**Status.** The authority gap remains open — nothing read establishes what Texas requires this oath to say. The text gap is closed: the wording actually used is known, consistent across three certified records, and available as a candidate for approval.

## Oath 4 — Witness affirmation

**When:** in place of Oath 3, when the witness declines to swear. Mutually exclusive with Oath 3.

**Administered by:** as Oath 3.

**Published wording: none.**

**A second and separate gap.** §3.16(a) lists `(The witness was affirmed)` as a recommended parenthetical, so the manual contemplates an affirming witness. But **every published setup figure says "sworn"**:

- Figure 14 — `having been first duly sworn, testified as follows:`
- Figure 15 — `having been first duly sworn, testified as follows:`
- Figure 16 — `having been first duly sworn, testified through the duly sworn interpreter as follows:`

And both certificate figures say `was duly sworn by the officer`.

So for a witness who affirms rather than swears, there is no published spoken text, **no published transcript setup line, and no published certificate line.** The manual gives a parenthetical and nothing to go with it.

**No specimen to recover from either.** All three records show a witness who swore. The affirmation has never been administered in the available evidence, so the route that closed Oath 3 does not close this one.

### Live defect confirmed in the application

This is not only a documentation gap. Checked against the working tree on 2026-08-29:

- `app/OpeningProceduresScreen.tsx` offers **Witness oath selection: Oath / Affirmation**, and the choice is persisted — `server/opening-procedures.mjs:123` validates it against `["UNRESOLVED","OATH","AFFIRMATION"]` and writes it to the opening state.
- Both Texas certification templates contain the sentence as a **literal string with no token and no conditional**:
  - `templates/insertion-pages/TEXAS_STATE_SIGNATURE_REQUESTED/certification-1.tmpl:13`
  - `templates/insertion-pages/TEXAS_STATE_SIGNATURE_WAIVED/certification-1.tmpl:13`
  - both read `That the witness, ^deposition.witness^, was duly sworn by the officer`
- `witnessOathSelection` has **no consumer outside `server/opening-procedures.mjs` and its tests.** No certificate code path reads it. The value cannot reach the renderer.

**Consequence.** A reporter who selects Affirmation, correctly recording that the witness declined to swear, still generates a certificate stating the witness was duly sworn — under the reporter's name, CSR number and signature. There is no guard against this and no test covering it.

**Currently latent.** No specimen used an affirmation, so no incorrect certificate is known to have been produced. It is a defect waiting on its first affirming witness, not one that has already fired.

This is independent of the sequencer work and independent of every open authority question in this report. It does not require knowing what the affirmation *should* say — only that the certificate must not assert an oath that was not administered.

## Oath 5 — Remote administration

Not a separate oath. A precondition on oaths 3 and 4 that the 2010 UFM cannot address, because it predates remote depositions entirely.

**The question:** may the officer administer an oath to a witness who is not physically present, and is counsel's stipulation required for that? If it is required and was not taken, then the certificate's assertion that the witness was duly sworn is contestable — which puts this in the same category as everything else in this inventory rather than beside it.

**Published wording: none.** No source read for this report speaks to remote administration. The manual is fifteen years older than the practice.

**Wording as administered: recovered, and consistent.** All three specimen depositions were taken remotely via Zoom, and all three take the stipulation on the record before the oath:

> THE REPORTER: Counsel, will you please state your agreement for this remote deposition and the remote swearing of the witness by stating your name, who you're representing, and the name of the city you're currently in, beginning with `[FIRST COUNSEL]`.

**CORRECTED.** The quotation above is the Baier wording. An earlier draft of this section presented it as though it were canonical. It is not: the stipulation has three genuine variants across three depositions, and unlike the oath it is not fixed to the character.

| File | Text |
|---|---|
| Baier | `who you're representing, and the name of the city you're currently in` |
| Etminan | `who you're representing, and the name of the city you are currently in` |
| Thomas | `who you're representing, the name of the city you are currently in` |

Contraction differs, and Thomas drops the `and`. The oath reads as recited from something; the stipulation reads as spoken extemporaneously. See findings F-14.

**Consequence.** If this step is templated, the template is a **new authored artifact**, not an extraction from practice, and must be recorded as such. The oath can be extracted. This cannot.

Each counsel then states name, party represented, and physical location. The oath follows. The reporter also states their own certification — "licensed in Texas, number 12129" — in the same passage.

**Tier:**

| What | Tier | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| That a remote oath is permissible | **UNDETERMINED** | TRCP 199.1(b), TRCP 203, any standing Supreme Court order. **None read.** |
| That counsel's stipulation is required | **UNDETERMINED** | as above |
| The wording | **CUSTOMARY** | No published form exists; practice is consistent across three records |

**Why UNDETERMINED and not CUSTOMARY.** The three tiers in the prompt pack describe how strongly authority speaks to *wording*. This row is a question about *permissibility*, which none of the three answers. It is marked undetermined rather than forced into a vocabulary that does not fit.

**Consequence for the application.** Modality belongs in the resolve phase alongside interpreter and oath selection, and the stipulation step is conditional on it — this is already the shape in the builder brief. What the brief does not carry is that the stipulation is **load-bearing for the certificate**, not merely a courtesy: it is the thing that makes "duly sworn by the officer" defensible when the officer and the witness were in different cities.

**Open, and the most consequential unread item in this report.** Whether the stipulation is legally required, merely prudent, or irrelevant is unresolved. Your practice takes it every time, which is the safe posture regardless of the answer.

## What the record asserts after the fact

Each oath produces claims further down the document chain. These are the sentences the spoken text has to be true of.

**Transcript setup line, deposition — Figure 15:**

> `^WITNESS NAME,`
> `having been first duly sworn, testified as follows:`
> `EXAMINATION`
> `BY ^ MR./MS. ^LAWYER:`

**Transcript setup line, interpreted deposition — Figure 16:**

> `^ WITNESS NAME,`
> `having been first duly sworn, testified through the duly sworn interpreter as follows:`

This one line asserts that **both** the witness and the interpreter were sworn.

**Certificate — Figures 8 and 9, identically:**

> `That the witness, ^WITNESS NAME, was duly sworn`
> `by the officer and that the transcript of the oral`
> `deposition is a true record of the testimony given by the`
> `witness;`

**Recommended parentheticals — §3.16(a):** `(Interpreter sworn)`, `(The witness was sworn)`, `(The witness was affirmed)`, `(Discussion between interpreter and witness)`.

**Consequence.** A skipped or unresolved oath step does not merely leave a gap. It causes three later documents to assert something that did not happen, one of them under the reporter's certification and CSR number. That is the evidentiary basis for refusing to advance past an unresolved oath step.

## Two facts about the certificate that bear on the oaths

**The officer is singular and unnamed.** Figures 8 and 9 both read "duly sworn by the officer" and close with one signature block: one name, one CSR number, one expiration date, one firm registration number. The published form cannot express a deposition where two officers staffed the room, and it does not name who administered the oath.

This is in tension with the §3.3 comment, which expressly contemplates more than one deposition officer, each certifying the form of recording they were responsible for. The tension is in the source, not introduced here.

**Depositions head the setup EXAMINATION, not DIRECT EXAMINATION.** Figure 14 (trial) and Figure 15 (deposition) are otherwise identical. Checkable against existing transcripts without touching code.

## Standing rule that applies to everything above

No Government Code section number taken from the 2010 UFM may be cited in a template, a certificate, or a compliance claim without independent confirmation of its current number. Every such citation in that manual points at Chapter 52; reporter regulation is now in Chapter 154. Treat manual-sourced Government Code citations as leads, never as citations.
